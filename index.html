    01 The Approach

    There is no road to K2

    K2 rises on the border between Pakistan-administered Gilgit-Baltistan and the Xinjiang region of China, deep in the Karakoram. From the Pakistani side, expeditions fly or drive to Skardu, take jeeps to the village of Askole, then walk for about a week up the Baltoro Glacier, a river of ice and rubble more than 60 km long.

    At Concordia, where the Baltoro meets the Godwin-Austen Glacier at about 4,600 m, the mountain finally comes into view: a near-perfect pyramid at the head of the valley.

    02 Base Camp · 5,000 m

    A mountain named by a surveyor's notebook

    In 1856 the Great Trigonometrical Survey of India sighted the high Karakoram from about 200 km away. Surveyor T. G. Montgomerie labelled the peaks K1, K2, K3 and so on, for Karakoram. The others later took local names; the second peak kept its number. It has also been called Chhogori and Mount Godwin-Austen.

    On 31 July 1954 Lino Lacedelli and Achille Compagnoni of Ardito Desio's Italian expedition reached the top. Below them, Walter Bonatti and the Hunza porter Amir Mahdi, who had carried up the summit pair's oxygen, were forced to spend the night in the open at about 8,100 m. Mahdi's toes were badly frostbitten. The official account of that night was disputed for half a century, until the Italian Alpine Club accepted Bonatti's version in 2007.

    03 The Climb

    Up the Abruzzi Spur

    Most ascents follow the Abruzzi Spur, the south-east ridge first tried by the Duke of the Abruzzi's expedition in 1909. Fixed ropes link four camps: past House's Chimney near Camp 2 and the Black Pyramid above it, up to Camp 4 on the broad Shoulder, pitched anywhere between about 7,600 m and 8,000 m.

    The amber line is the Abruzzi; the cyan line is the Cesen route, which climbs the next spur to the west and joins it at the Shoulder.

    04 The Bottleneck · 8,200 m

    Under the serac

    Above the Shoulder the route narrows into the Bottleneck, a steep couloir around 8,200 m, then traverses left beneath a serac: a cliff of glacier ice that hangs over the route and can break without warning. Climbers usually go through in the dark, hours before dawn.

    On 1 August 2008 a block of ice broke from the serac here, killing a climber and cutting the fixed ropes while others were still above. Eleven people died over about two days, including two who fell earlier on the crowded ascent: the worst single disaster in K2's history.

    Above 8,000 m, the "death zone", each breath holds about a third of the oxygen it would at sea level.

    05 Summit · 8,611 m

    The last of the fourteen

    For decades K2 was the only one of the fourteen 8,000-metre peaks that had never been climbed in winter. On 16 January 2021, ten Nepali climbers, among them Nirmal "Nims" Purja and Mingma Gyalje Sherpa ("Mingma G"), regrouped just below the top and walked the last metres together, singing the Nepali national anthem. Purja died in July 2026, one of ten climbers killed by an avalanche on neighbouring Broad Peak.

    From the top, the Karakoram falls away on every side: Broad Peak and the Gasherbrums to the south-east, China's Shaksgam valley to the north.
